
# Verified Research Papers

This collection contains verified scholarly papers related to human trust calibration, appropriate reliance, human-AI collaboration, autonomous agents, and autonomous scientific research.

**Verification principle:** a reference is included only after checking that the cited publication exists and that its DOI or persistent identifier corresponds to the same work. The collection deliberately distinguishes foundational peer-reviewed work from recent preprints.

## Survey and Review Papers

### 1. Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance
John D. Lee, Katrina A. See, 2004, *Human Factors*, 46(1), 50–80.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392)  
Foundational review explaining why the goal should be appropriate reliance rather than maximum trust.

### 2. A Meta-Analysis of Factors Affecting Trust in Human-Robot Interaction
Peter A. Hancock, Deborah R. Billings, Kristin E. Schaefer, Jessie Y. C. Chen, Ewart J. de Visser, Raja Parasuraman, 2011, *Human Factors*, 53(5), 517–527.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720811417254)  
Quantifies human, robot, and environmental factors associated with trust in HRI.

### 3. Trust in Automation: Integrating Empirical Evidence on Factors That Influence Trust
Kevin A. Hoff, Masooda Bashir, 2015, *Human Factors*, 57(3), 407–434.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720814547570)  
Synthesizes empirical evidence into a dispositional, situational, and learned trust model.

### 4. Trust Measurement in Human–Automation Interaction: A Systematic Review
Matthew Brzowski, Dan Nathan-Roberts, 2019, *Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society Annual Meeting*, 63(1).  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1071181319631462)  
Reviews how trust is measured and highlights limitations of subjective and researcher-defined measures.

### 5. Trust, Distrust, and Appropriate Reliance in (X)AI: A Conceptual Clarification of User Trust and Survey of Its Empirical Evaluation
Roel Visser, Tobias M. Peters, Ingrid Scharlau, Barbara Hammer, 2025, *Cognitive Systems Research*, 91, 101357.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogsys.2025.101357)  
Clarifies trust, distrust, and appropriate reliance and surveys empirical work on XAI.

### 6. A Survey on Large Language Model Based Autonomous Agents
Lei Wang, Chen Ma, Xueyang Feng, Zeyu Zhang, Hao Yang, et al., 2024, *Frontiers of Computer Science*, 18, 186345.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11704-024-40231-1)  
Surveys architectures and capabilities of autonomous LLM agents relevant to research workflows.

## Foundational Papers

### 7. Trust in Automation: Part I. Theoretical Issues in the Study of Trust and Human Intervention in Automated Systems
Bonnie M. Muir, 1994, *Ergonomics*, 37(11), 1905–1922.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140139408964957)  
Provides an early theoretical account of trust and intervention in automated systems.

### 8. Humans and Automation: Use, Misuse, Disuse, Abuse
Raja Parasuraman, Victor Riley, 1997, *Human Factors*, 39(2), 230–253.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872097778543886)  
Defines misuse, disuse, and abuse and explains how trust and workload influence automation use.

### 9. Foundations for an Empirically Determined Scale of Trust in Automated Systems
Jiun-Yin Jian, Ann M. Bisantz, Colin G. Drury, 2000, *International Journal of Cognitive Ergonomics*, 4(1), 53–71.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327566IJCE0401_04)  
Develops an empirically based scale for measuring trust in automated systems.

### 10. Beyond Accuracy: The Role of Mental Models in Human-AI Team Performance
Gagan Bansal, Besmira Nushi, Ece Kamar, Walter S. Lasecki, Daniel S. Weld, Eric Horvitz, 2019, *Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Human Computation and Crowdsourcing*, 7(1), 2–11.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1609/hcomp.v7i1.5285)  
Shows that knowing an AI's capabilities and error boundary is important for effective human-AI teamwork.

### 11. Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction
Saleema Amershi, Dan Weld, Mihaela Vorvoreanu, Adam Fourney, et al., 2019, *Proceedings of the CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems*.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233)  
Presents 18 general guidelines for designing human-AI interaction.

## Trust Calibration and Human-Agent Collaboration

### 12. Trust Calibration within a Human-Robot Team: Comparing Automatically Generated Explanations
Ning Wang, David V. Pynadath, Susan G. Hill, 2016, *2016 ACM/IEEE International Conference on Human-Robot Interaction*, 109–116.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/HRI.2016.7451741)  
Examines the effect of automatically generated explanations on trust calibration in human-robot teams.

### 13. Adaptive Trust Calibration for Human-AI Collaboration
Kazuo Okamura, Seiji Yamada, 2020, *PLOS ONE*, 15(2), e0229132.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229132)  
Introduces adaptive trust-calibration cues based on user reliance behavior and evaluates them in a drone inspection task.

### 14. Trust in AI: Why We Should Be Designing for Appropriate Reliance
Natalie C. Benda, Laurie L. Novak, Carrie Reale, Jessica S. Ancker, 2021, *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association*, 29(1), 207–212.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocab238)  
Applies appropriate-reliance principles to AI decision support and emphasizes dynamic trust.

### 15. An Explanation Is Not an Excuse: Trust Calibration in an Age of Transparent Robots
Alan R. Wagner, Paul Robinette, 2021, in *Trust in Human-Robot Interaction*, Academic Press, pp. 197–208.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-819472-0.00009-5)  
Explains why transparency and explanations do not automatically produce calibrated trust.

### 16. How the Different Explanation Classes Impact Trust Calibration: The Case of Clinical Decision Support Systems
Mohammad Naiseh, Dena Al-Thani, Nan Jiang, Raian Ali, 2023, *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies*, 169, 102941.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2022.102941)  
Evaluates explanation classes and reports that explanations can sometimes increase AI over-reliance.

### 17. Calibrating Trust, Reliance and Dependence in Variable-Reliability Automation
Christopher Holland, Grace Perry, Heather F. Neyedli, 2024, *Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society Annual Meeting*, 68(1), 604–610.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/10711813241277531)  
Studies trust and dependence when automation reliability changes over time.

### 18. Trust and Reliance on AI — An Experimental Study on the Extent and Costs of Overreliance on AI
Artur Klingbeil, Cassandra Grützner, Philipp Schreck, 2024, *Computers in Human Behavior*, 160, 108352.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2024.108352)  
Provides behavioral evidence of AI over-reliance and its consequences for decision quality.

### 19. Investigating Appropriate Reliance on AI-Based Decision Support Systems: The Role of Expertise, Trust, and Self-Confidence
Enis Ömer Dogru, Nicole C. Krämer, 2025, *Journal of Decision Systems*, 34(1), Article 2593251.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/12460125.2025.2593251)  
Examines how expertise, trust, and self-confidence jointly influence appropriate reliance.

### 20. Examining Human Reliance on Artificial Intelligence in Decision Making
Joe Pearson, Itiel E. Dror, Emma Jayes, Grace-Rose Whordley, Georgina Mason, Sophie Nightingale, 2026, *Scientific Reports*, 16, 5345.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-34983-y)  
Recent experimental work showing that attitudes toward AI can influence reliance and decision performance.

## Autonomous Research and AI Agents

### 21. The AI Scientist: Towards Fully Automated Open-Ended Scientific Discovery
Chris Lu, Cong Lu, Robert Tjarko Lange, Jakob Foerster, Jeff Clune, David Ha, 2024, *arXiv preprint arXiv:2408.06292*.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2408.06292)  
Presents an end-to-end autonomous scientific discovery workflow that generates ideas, writes code, runs experiments, and produces papers.

### 22. Towards an AI Co-Scientist
Juraj Gottweis, Wei-Hung Weng, Alexander Daryin, Tao Tu, Anil Palepu, et al., 2025, *arXiv preprint arXiv:2502.18864*.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.18864)  
Introduces a multi-agent AI co-scientist designed to generate and refine scientific hypotheses with researchers.

### 23. Detecting Hallucinations in Large Language Models Using Semantic Entropy
Sebastian Farquhar, Jannik Kossen, Lorenz Kuhn, Yarin Gal, 2024, *Nature*, 630, 625–630.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07421-0)  
Presents semantic entropy as a method for detecting uncertainty associated with hallucinated LLM outputs.

### 24. To Trust or Not to Trust? An Assessment of Trust in AI-Based Systems: Concerns, Ethics and Contexts
Nessrine Omrani, Giorgia Rivieccio, Ugo Fiore, Francesco Schiavone, Sergio Garcia Agreda, 2022, *Technological Forecasting and Social Change*, 181, 121763.  
[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2022.121763)  
Reviews trust in AI across organizational and ethical contexts and highlights factors affecting trust formation.
